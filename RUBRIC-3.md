# Rubric 3 — visitor planning and optional details

Approved for the next map update. Saved separately from RUBRIC.md to preserve the earlier rubric. Publishing this rubric does not itself change the maps.

## Who and when

For first-time visitors planning a day trip with family or friends: at home to choose stops, then on a phone at the park to understand what comes next.

## Score each park out of 20

| Criterion | Points | Full marks require |
|---|---:|---|
| Find things quickly | /5 | Entrances, camps, sights, and visitor centers are easy to find on a phone. Names and symbols are readable; search and filters help. |
| Choose a realistic visit | /5 | Each stop includes visit time and walking effort. Suggested trips connect sensible stops and include driving allowances. Each place explains who it is recommended for and when someone might skip it. |
| Trust and learn | /5 | Accurate connections, distinguished access types, dated sources, current-condition links, clearly identified estimates, and one verified, sourced fun fact per marked place. |
| Comfortable and appealing to use | /5 | Welcoming design, readable text, touch-sized controls, keyboard access, and useful phone behavior. Each place has a clearly labeled "More information" button to show or hide optional details. |
| **Total** | **/20** | Include one honest weakness per park. |

## Extra information for every marked place

Applies to viewpoints, landmarks, trails, campgrounds, entrances, and visitor centers.

- **Recommended if…:** Explain who would enjoy or benefit from the place, and why.
- **Consider skipping if…:** Explain a meaningful limitation, such as a steep walk, rough access road, limited time, or interests better served elsewhere. Do not invent a drawback simply to fill the field.
- **Fun fact:** Include one interesting, verified detail with a source link. For practical places such as entrances, a useful verified detail is acceptable instead of forced trivia.

## Required checks, separate from the score

- **R5 — Symbols:** Each marker carries its category's legend symbol. Use symbols and words, not color alone.
- **R6 — Explore comfortably:** Every place and name is reachable on a phone. Provide drag-to-pan, wheel/pinch zoom, zoom buttons, reset, and a searchable place list.
- **R7 — Connected maps:** Each park has one continuous map, not disconnected quadrant drawings. Every place connects by a road, marked trail, or clearly identified shuttle connection. Include outside access roads where needed; never invent a through-road. Shared park navigation preserves saved stops.
- **R8 — Useful planning:** Include entrances/access routes and campgrounds, or explain when developed camping is not available. Show walking effort, visit time, and travel allowances between stops. Date closure notes and link their sources; static information must never look live.
- **R9 — Optional extra details:** Each place has a button initially labeled **More information**. Optional details start hidden. Opening it reveals the recommendation, reasons to consider skipping, fun fact, and supporting sources in the same place. The button changes to **Less information** and lets visitors hide the details again. It must work with touch and keyboard and tell screen readers whether the details are open or closed. A screen reader is a tool that reads the page aloud.

Names, essential visit/walking information, access restrictions, and important closure or safety warnings stay visible without opening "More information." Optional details must not crowd the map itself.

A failed required check means a map is not release-ready, regardless of its /20 score. Use official NPS maps and current conditions for actual navigation and travel decisions.

## Honest trade-off

Hiding extra details keeps the page shorter, but visitors may miss them unless the button is easy to notice. Essential safety and access information must therefore remain visible.
