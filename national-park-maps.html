🚪 West Entrance Station

Park entrance

Main west gate — pay the entrance fee here.

🎉 Fun fact: This is the park's busiest entrance — most visitors come in this way.

Time needed: About 5–10 min (estimate) — a drive-through stop to pay the fee or show your pass. On busy weekend and holiday mornings there can be a line (30+ min, estimate).

Walking: No real walk — a ranger booth on Park Boulevard in the town of Joshua Tree. You stay in the car.

✅ Recommended: Best entrance if you're staying in Joshua Tree town; it's the quickest way to the famous rock sights.

🚪 North Entrance Station

Park entrance

North gate near Twentynine Palms.

🎉 Fun fact: From here, one road runs all the way south through the park to Cottonwood.

Time needed: About 5–10 min (estimate) — the same drive-through idea.

Walking: No real walk — a ranger booth on Utah Trail, south of Twentynine Palms.

✅ Recommended: Best entrance for Cholla Cactus Garden and the quieter south side.

🚪 South Entrance (Cottonwood)

Park entrance

South gate — closest to Interstate 10.

🎉 Fun fact: This side of the park is the Colorado Desert — lower, hotter, and full of cholla and ocotillo instead of Joshua trees.

Time needed: About 5–10 min (estimate); lines are possible on weekend mornings.

Walking: No real walk — an entrance station on the road up from Interstate 10 (exit 168), next to Cottonwood Spring; the Cottonwood Visitor Center is just inside.

✅ Recommended: Best entrance if you're driving from Phoenix or Palm Springs on I-10; it's also the least crowded.

ℹ️ Joshua Tree Visitor Center

Visitor center

Downtown Joshua Tree — maps, rangers, exhibits.

🎉 Fun fact: This visitor center sits in the town of Joshua Tree, right outside the park's west entrance.

Time needed: 20–30 min (estimate) — look at exhibits, grab a free park map, ask a ranger. Open 7:30 am–5 pm daily.

Walking: No real walk — in downtown Joshua Tree (6554 Park Blvd), just outside the West Entrance. Indoors, plus a short stroll.

✅ Recommended: Stop here first for maps and ranger tips before you drive in.

ℹ️ Oasis of Mara Visitor Center

Visitor center

By the North Entrance in Twentynine Palms.

🎉 Fun fact: The oasis next door has been a water stop for people for thousands of years — the Serrano people called it Mara.

Time needed: 20–30 min (estimate).

Walking: No real walk inside; outside is the Oasis of Mara — a grove of California fan palms with a short paved path (about a 0.5-mile loop, Easy).

Heads-up: NPS lists the old Oasis Visitor Center building here (74485 National Park Dr, Twentynine Palms) as permanently closed. The park's Twentynine Palms visitor center moved downtown to 6533 Freedom Way (open 8 am–5 pm). The palm oasis and its short trail are still here to visit.

✅ Recommended: Great first stop if you enter from the north; it has a short nature trail to the oasis.

ℹ️ Cottonwood Visitor Center

Visitor center

South end of the park.

🎉 Fun fact: Cottonwood Spring nearby was a lifeline for travelers crossing the desert — people have used it for centuries.

Time needed: 20–30 min (estimate). Open 8:30 am–4 pm daily.

Walking: No real walk — a small center on Pinto Basin Road, about 7 miles north of I-10 exit 168, right by the South Entrance. Picnic tables nearby.

✅ Recommended: Good stop for the south end; ask rangers about the spring trail.

🔭 Keys View

Viewpoint

The big panorama — on clear days you can see all the way to Mexico.

🎉 Fun fact: On a really clear day you can see the San Andreas Fault, the Coachella Valley, and Signal Mountain in Mexico.

Time needed: 15–30 min.

Walking: No real walk — a drive-up overlook at the very end of Keys View Road; a short paved path from the parking lot to the railing.

✅ Recommended: The best sunset spot in the park — go late in the day.

🔭 Ryan Mountain Summit

Viewpoint

360° views from the top — a steep 3-mile round-trip hike.

🎉 Fun fact: At 5,457 feet, this is one of the highest viewpoints in the park.

Time needed: 2–3 hrs (the hike up and down).

Walking: Hard — 3-mile round trip, climbs about 1,000 ft. NPS calls the trail “moderately strenuous” — it is the steepest walk on this map, and there is no shade. Bring lots of water.

✅ Recommended: Recommended if you like a challenge — but not on a very hot day, since there's no shade on the trail.

⛺ Black Rock Campground

Campground

Tent and RV camping on the park's west side.

🎉 Fun fact: This campground has water faucets — rare in this desert park — and a nature center.

Time needed: Most people stay overnight — that is the point of a campground. Driving through for a quick look: 15–20 min (estimate).

Walking: No real walk — a campground in the park's northwest corner, reached by Black Rock Canyon Road from Yucca Valley. 99 sites; water, flush toilets, and a dump station — the west side's full-service campground.

Booking: Reserve ahead on recreation.gov.

✅ Recommended: Best pick for first-timers; it has water and is close to town.

⛺ Indian Cove Campground

Campground

Camp among giant boulders — a climber favorite.

🎉 Fun fact: The boulders here are some of the best rock-climbing spots in the park, even for beginners.

Time needed: Most people stay overnight — that is the point of a campground. Driving through for a quick look: 15–20 min (estimate).

Walking: No real walk — a campground in its own separate area off Indian Cove Road (from Hwy 62, on the Twentynine Palms side). It does not connect to Park Boulevard by car. 101 sites, no water — bring your own.

Booking: Reserve ahead on recreation.gov.

✅ Recommended: Recommended for climbers; not the best pick if you mainly want to see big Joshua trees, since this area is more about boulders.

⛺ Jumbo Rocks Campground

Campground

The classic — huge rock piles right by your tent.

🎉 Fun fact: The giant rock piles formed over 100 million years ago, deep underground — erosion uncovered them.

Time needed: Most people stay overnight — that is the point of a campground. Driving through for a quick look: 15–20 min (estimate).

Walking: No real walk — a campground right on Park Boulevard, with Skull Rock just across the road. Biggest in the park: 124 sites. No water.

Booking: Reserve ahead on recreation.gov.

✅ Recommended: The most famous campground in the park — book early, it fills up fast.

⛺ Ryan Campground

Campground

Small, quiet campground near Ryan Mountain.

🎉 Fun fact: It's one of the smallest campgrounds in the park, so it feels extra quiet at night.

Time needed: Most people stay overnight — that is the point of a campground. Driving through for a quick look: 15–20 min (estimate).

Walking: No real walk — a small campground (31 sites) on Park Boulevard, close to the Ryan Mountain trailhead. No water.

Booking: Reserve ahead on recreation.gov.

✅ Recommended: Recommended if you want quiet and a short walk to the Ryan Mountain trail.

⛺ Belle Campground

Campground

Small campground tucked among boulders and Joshua trees.

🎉 Fun fact: Joshua Tree is a certified Dark Sky Park, so the stars here are amazing — no city lights.

Time needed: Most people stay overnight — that is the point of a campground. Driving through for a quick look: 15–20 min (estimate).

Walking: No real walk — a small campground (18 sites) on Pinto Basin Road near the Park Boulevard junction. No water.

Booking: First-come, first-served, $25/night. Closed in summer (about June 1–Aug 28).

✅ Recommended: Great for stargazing; small and peaceful.

⛺ White Tank Campground

Campground

Small and scenic, next to Arch Rock.

🎉 Fun fact: It's named after a natural white rock basin nearby that fills with rainwater for desert animals.

Time needed: Most people stay overnight — that is the point of a campground. Driving through for a quick look: 15–20 min (estimate).

Walking: No real walk — a small, quiet campground (15 sites) on Pinto Basin Road, right by the Arch Rock trail. No water.

Booking: First-come, first-served, $25/night. Closed in summer (about June 1–Aug 28).

✅ Recommended: Recommended if you want to walk to Arch Rock at sunrise.

⛺ Cottonwood Campground

Campground

South-end campground by the visitor center.

🎉 Fun fact: This campground is in the Colorado Desert, so you'll see cholla cacti and ocotillo instead of Joshua trees.

Time needed: Most people stay overnight — that is the point of a campground. Driving through for a quick look: 15–20 min (estimate).

Walking: No real walk — a campground (62 sites) on Pinto Basin Road by the Cottonwood Visitor Center. Water, flush toilets, and a dump station. Famous for dark skies.

Booking: Reserve ahead on recreation.gov.

✅ Recommended: Recommended if you're driving in from I-10 and want a quiet night.

🌵 Skull Rock

Famous sight

A rock that looks exactly like a skull. Easy roadside stop.

🎉 Fun fact: Rain and wind hollowed out two 'eye sockets' in the rock — that's what makes it look like a skull.

Time needed: 10–30 min for the roadside stop.

Walking: No real walk — the giant rock that looks like a skull sits right beside Park Boulevard, across from Jumbo Rocks Campground; you can see it from the car. Optional extra: the Skull Rock nature trail — Easy, 1.7-mile loop, 45 min–1 hr.

✅ Recommended: A must-stop: it's right by the road and takes 2 minutes.

🌵 Arch Rock

Famous sight

Natural stone arch — short easy trail from White Tank.

🎉 Fun fact: The arch formed when softer rock underneath wore away, leaving the harder rock on top.

Time needed: 30–45 min.

Walking: Easy — the Arch Rock Nature Trail, a short loop off Pinto Basin Road (Twin Tanks parking lot). About 1.2 miles round trip from the parking lot; the loop at the rock itself is about 0.5 mile. Very little climbing.

✅ Recommended: Easy and quick — a great photo stop.

🌵 Heart Rock

Famous sight

A giant boulder shaped like a heart, a short walk past Arch Rock.

🎉 Fun fact: It's a giant boulder about 10 feet tall — and at certain times of day, the sun throws a giant heart-shaped shadow.

Time needed: Add ~15 min on top of Arch Rock (estimate); Arch Rock + Heart Rock together ≈ 45 min–1 hr.

Walking: Easy — the same Arch Rock Trail; Heart Rock is just past Arch Rock. Doing both from the parking lot ≈ 1.7 miles round trip. (Estimate — there is no separate NPS trail page for Heart Rock.)

✅ Recommended: A favorite photo stop — pair it with Arch Rock on the same short trail.

🌵 Barker Dam

Famous sight

A dam built by early cattle ranchers; 1.1-mile loop with ancient rock carvings.

🎉 Fun fact: The dam was built around 1900 so cattle could drink here — and bighorn sheep still drink here today.

Time needed: 30–45 min.

Walking: Easy — 1.1-mile loop. Flat, with a few rock steps; you pass the old cattle dam, petroglyphs (rock art), and big boulders. Water in the dam only shows up after rainy seasons.

✅ Recommended: One of the best easy hikes; you might spot bighorn sheep.

🌵 Cholla Cactus Garden

Famous sight

A forest of teddy-bear cholla cacti — look, don't touch!

🎉 Fun fact: Cholla pieces break off and stick to anything that brushes them — that's why people call it the 'jumping cactus' (it doesn't really jump).

Time needed: 15–30 min.

Walking: Easy — 0.25-mile loop, flat, partly on a boardwalk, on Pinto Basin Road. Stay on the path — the spines grab shoes and clothes.

✅ Recommended: Recommended, but stay on the path — the spines are barbed and hurt.

🌵 Hidden Valley

Famous sight

A hidden valley ringed by giant rocks; easy 1-mile loop.

🎉 Fun fact: Cattle rustlers once hid stolen cows in this valley because the rocks hid it from view.

Time needed: 30–45 min.

Walking: Easy — 1-mile loop. A flat sandy valley ringed by rock piles, right off Park Boulevard. Picnic area at the parking lot.

✅ Recommended: A classic easy walk — great for first-time visitors.

🌵 Hall of Horrors

Famous sight

Narrow passages between boulders — fun to squeeze through.

🎉 Fun fact: It's named for the tight squeezes between the boulders — you have to wiggle through some gaps.

Time needed: 30–60 min (longer if you explore).

Walking: Easy walk — a 0.6-mile loop around the rock piles off Park Boulevard — but the famous part is two narrow slot canyons: tight squeezes and rock scrambling. That part is more like Moderate, and not fun if you dislike small spaces.

❌ Not recommended: Not recommended if you don't like tight spaces — you have to squeeze between boulders.

🌵 Cap Rock

Famous sight

Famous balanced rock formation right by the road.

🎉 Fun fact: A huge boulder balances on a narrow base — it looks like it should fall over, but it's been there for ages.

Time needed: 15–30 min (estimate).

Walking: Easy — about a 0.4-mile loop trail around a boulder with a rock “cap” on top, at the corner of Park Boulevard and Keys View Road. Interpretive signs along the path.

✅ Recommended: Right by the road, so it's an easy stop.

🥾 Ryan Mountain Trailhead

Trailhead

Start of the steep 3-mile round-trip hike to the summit.

🎉 Fun fact: The trail climbs about 1,000 feet — that's like climbing 100 flights of stairs.

Time needed: 2–3 hrs (the hike up and down).

Walking: Hard — 3-mile round trip, climbs about 1,000 ft, no shade. The parking lot is on Park Boulevard, between the Ryan and Sheep Pass campgrounds.

✅ Recommended: Recommended for the best views — but bring lots of water and skip it on very hot days.

🥾 Fortynine Palms Oasis Trailhead

Trailhead

3-mile round trip to a hidden palm oasis.

🎉 Fun fact: The fan palms at the oasis are California fan palms — the only palm tree native to California.

Time needed: 2–3 hrs.

Walking: Moderate — 3-mile round trip. Climbs ~300 ft over a ridge, then drops into a canyon to a real palm oasis — and you climb back out. Almost no shade; bring extra water. Trailhead on Canyon Road off Highway 62 near Twentynine Palms (not on Park Boulevard). Day-use only — the gate locks at night.

Heads-up: This trail is closed every summer, June 1–Sept 30.

✅ Recommended: Recommended for a real hidden oasis — just don't go at midday in summer, the trail has almost no shade.

🥾 Lost Horse Mine Trailhead

Trailhead

4-mile round trip to a real old gold mine.

🎉 Fun fact: This mine produced about 10,000 ounces of gold before it closed.

Time needed: 2–3 hrs (NPS's trail list says 3–4 hrs if you take your time and explore the longer loop).

Walking: Moderate — 4-mile round trip on an old wagon road to a real gold mine and ten-stamp mill; a longer loop option is about 6.5 miles. Trailhead at the end of a short dirt road off Keys View Road.

✅ Recommended: Recommended if you like history — it's one of the best-preserved mines in the park.

Trip combos — stops that fit together

Nearby stops that make one good outing, in the order you reach them on the road.

🧭 Park Boulevard Classics

West Entrance → Hidden Valley → Barker Dam → Cap Rock → Keys View

Stop time ≈ 2–2.5 hrs total (plus driving).

Why they fit: Everything sits on Park Boulevard or its one side road, Keys View Road, in the park's northwest — one road, no backtracking.

🧭 East-Side Rock Stars

Skull Rock → Arch Rock → Heart Rock → Cholla Cactus Garden

Stop time ≈ 1.5–2 hrs total.

Why they fit: Drive east on Park Boulevard, then turn onto Pinto Basin Road — the stops come in exactly this order along the two roads, ending further south. (Arch Rock + Heart Rock are one trail stop.)

🧭 North Gate Morning

Oasis of Mara → Fortynine Palms Oasis Trail

Total ≈ 2.5–3.5 hrs.

Why they fit: Both are at Twentynine Palms by the North Entrance, off Highway 62 — do the short oasis stop first, then the bigger hike; the trailhead is a few miles west on Canyon Road. Skip Fortynine Palms in summer — it is closed June–September.

Before you go

Stay safe: This is desert. Many trails have almost no shade — carry plenty of water, start hikes early, and skip the steep climbs (like Ryan Mountain) on very hot days.

Sources: National Park Service — nps.gov/jotr. Info checked: October 2026.

🏜️ Ubehebe Crater

Famous sight

A huge volcanic crater in the far north of the park.

Fun fact: The crater was formed by steam-powered volcanic explosions, not by a meteor.

Time needed: 30–60 min. Just looking into the crater from the parking lot takes ~15 min; walking the rim takes the rest.

Walking: Moderate — 1.5-mile loop around the rim (500 ft up/down). Easy option: the Little Hebe spur is shorter, ~0.9 mi round trip. No restrooms at the parking lot.

Recommended: A dramatic stop with a rim view and room to explore on foot.

⛺ Mesquite Spring Campground

Campground

A remote campground near the park’s northern sights.

Fun fact: Its north-park location makes it a useful base for Ubehebe Crater.

Time needed: Most people stay overnight. A quick look-around takes 15–20 min.

Walking: No real walk — a campground at 1,800 ft elevation, 2 miles off Scotty's Castle Road. 40 sites, flush toilets, water, fire grates. Open year-round.

Booking: First-come, first-served (no reservations).

Recommended: Choose it when the north end is your main destination; check current NPS conditions first.

🏜️ Racetrack Playa

Famous sight

A remote dry lakebed famous for rocks that leave long tracks.

Fun fact: Thin ice, water, wind, and mud can combine to move rocks across the playa.

Time needed: Half to a full day. The 27-mile gravel road each way takes 1.5–2.5 hrs. Estimate: plan ~5–6 hrs from Ubehebe Crater, a full day from Furnace Creek.

Walking: No real hike — the famous “sailing stones” sit on a flat dry lakebed; you stroll from the parking area (Easy, flat). NPS recommends a high-clearance 4-wheel-drive vehicle. No cell service — bring extra water and a full spare tire.

Not for a quick trip: Skip it on a quick trip—the road is remote and rough, so it needs careful planning.

🔭 Father Crowley Vista Point

Viewpoint

A west-side overlook above Rainbow Canyon.

Fun fact: The viewpoint is also a ranger-suggested place for dark-sky viewing.

Time needed: 15–20 min for the viewpoint; 30–45 min if you walk the dirt path.

Walking: No real walk — the view is right by the paved parking lot (restrooms there). Optional: an Easy 1.2-mile out-and-back dirt path to Padre Point, ~25 min.

Recommended: A strong west-side panorama, especially if your route enters from Panamint Springs.

⛺ Stovepipe Wells Campground

Campground

A campground near the Stovepipe Wells area.

Fun fact: It sits close to the Mesquite Flat Sand Dunes and Mosaic Canyon area.

Time needed: Most people stay overnight.

Walking: No real walk — a big flat campground at sea level, next to a small village with gas, food, and a store. 190 sites, water and flush toilets. Open in the cooler months, roughly fall through spring (estimate on exact dates).

Booking: First-come, first-served (no reservations).

Recommended: A practical base for the central-west sights; check current NPS conditions first.

🏜️ Mesquite Flat Sand Dunes

Famous sight

A broad field of wind-shaped dunes near Stovepipe Wells.

Fun fact: Low-angle morning or evening light makes the dune shapes stand out.

Time needed: 1–2 hrs (wander as far as you like — there is no marked trail).

Walking: Easy — up to ~2 miles round trip through sand. Sand walking is tiring, so go at your own pace. Best at sunrise or sunset; midday heat is brutal.

Recommended: One of the park’s easiest big landscapes to reach from the road.

🥾 Mosaic Canyon Trailhead

Trailhead

The start of a walk into a narrow, polished canyon.

Fun fact: Flash floods smoothed the canyon walls into marble-like curves.

Time needed: 2.5–3 hrs for the full hike; the first pretty narrows are under 0.5 mi in, so a short taste takes ~1 hr.

Walking: Moderate — 4-mile round trip out and back (1,200 ft of climbing, some scrambling over rocks). Trailhead at the end of a 2.3-mile unpaved road (fine for regular cars, not big RVs). Do NOT enter if rain is possible — flash floods.

Recommended: A memorable family hike; turn around whenever the canyon becomes too demanding.

🥾 Salt Creek Interpretive Trail

Trailhead

A boardwalk trail beside a desert creek.

Fun fact: Tiny pupfish live here; spring is a common time to see them.

Time needed: 30 min.

Walking: Easy — 0.5-mile boardwalk loop, flat and wheelchair accessible. Look for the rare Salt Creek pupfish in the water. The boardwalk reopened in Feb 2026 after flood damage. Almost no shade.

Recommended: A short, unusual stop that shows Death Valley is not completely dry.

🏜️ Harmony Borax Works

Famous sight

Historic remains of a short-lived borax-processing site.

Fun fact: Borax was processed here in the 1880s and hauled by twenty-mule-team wagons.

Time needed: 30–45 min.

Walking: Easy — 0.4-mile interpretive loop past old borax mining ruins — old wagons, tanks, and story signs. Right by Furnace Creek.

Recommended: An easy history stop close to Furnace Creek.

ℹ️ Furnace Creek Visitor Center

Visitor center

The park’s main place for maps, exhibits, and ranger advice.

Fun fact: It is the best place to check road, trail, heat, and campground conditions in person.

Time needed: 45 min–1 hr (a 20-min park film plays every half hour). Open daily 8 AM–5 PM.

Walking: No real walk — indoor exhibits, rangers to ask questions, water and a bookstore. Air-conditioned.

Recommended: Make this your first stop before exploring the lower valley.

⛺ Furnace Creek Campground

Campground

A central campground close to the visitor center.

Fun fact: Its location puts many famous lower-valley stops within a short drive.

Time needed: Most people stay overnight.

Walking: No real walk — a big campground 196 ft below sea level. 136 sites; water, flush toilets, dump station.

Booking: The only campground in the park you can reserve — Oct 15–Apr 15 on recreation.gov; first-come in summer. $22/night ($36 with hookups).

Recommended: A convenient base when you want the Furnace Creek area nearby; check current NPS conditions first.

⛺ Texas Springs Campground

Campground

A campground on the hills above Furnace Creek.

Fun fact: Its higher position looks back across the Furnace Creek area.

Time needed: Most people stay overnight.

Walking: No real walk — a hillside campground above Furnace Creek. 92 sites, water and flush toilets, no generators allowed; the lower loop is tents only. Open in the cooler months — roughly Oct–May (estimate).

Booking: First-come, first-served (no reservations).

Recommended: Good for a central base; check current NPS conditions before counting on a site.

🔭 Zabriskie Point

Viewpoint

A paved overlook above rippled golden badlands.

Fun fact: The eroded hills were built from lake sediments laid down long before today’s valley formed.

Time needed: 20–30 min.

Walking: No real hike — a short paved uphill trail from the parking lot to the viewpoint (about 0.25–0.4 mi round trip, estimate on exact length). Wheelchair-accessible. Famous sunrise spot.

Recommended: A must-see overlook with a short walk from the parking area.

🥾 Golden Canyon Trailhead

Trailhead

The starting point for trails into Golden Canyon and the badlands.

Fun fact: Trail connections can lead toward Red Cathedral, Gower Gulch, and Zabriskie Point.

Time needed: 1.5–2 hrs to Red Cathedral and back; longer loops (Gower Gulch 4.3 mi, ~2.5 hrs) are possible.

Walking: Moderate — 3-mile round trip to Red Cathedral (577 ft of gentle climbing, a little rock scrambling). Trailhead on Badwater Road, 2 miles south of CA-190.

Recommended: Great when temperatures are safe and you want to enter the badlands on foot.

🏜️ Artist’s Palette

Famous sight

Multicolored hills along Artist’s Drive.

Fun fact: Different minerals tint the slopes green, pink, yellow, and red.

Time needed: ~1 hr total (the drive plus stops).

Walking: No real hike — the colorful hills are right by the parking area; a short 0.3-mi stroll gets you closer. Reached via Artist's Drive, a 9-mile one-way paved scenic loop off Badwater Road. Best colors in late afternoon light. Vehicles over 25 ft are not allowed on the loop.

Recommended: A colorful road stop that pairs well with the lower Badwater Road sights.

🏜️ Devils Golf Course

Famous sight

A rough field of jagged salt formations.

Fun fact: Salt crystals grow into sharp, knobby shapes across the valley floor.

Time needed: 20–30 min.

Walking: No real hike — wander carefully a few yards from the parking area among the sharp salt pinnacles. Reached by a 1.3-mile unpaved access road off Badwater Road (rough but OK for cars — drive slowly). The salt spikes are razor sharp — stay on the edges and watch your step.

Recommended: Worth a careful look, but stay cautious around the sharp salt.

🥾 Natural Bridge Trailhead

Trailhead

The start of a canyon walk to a natural rock bridge.

Fun fact: Water erosion carved the canyon and left the bridge spanning overhead.

Time needed: 30–60 min (90 min if you keep walking past the bridge into the canyon).

Walking: Easy — 1-mile round trip on a gravel wash to the natural rock bridge (gentle uphill; you can continue ~1 more mile). Reached by a short unpaved road off Badwater Road, fine for sedans. The canyon walls give some shade.

Recommended: A short canyon adventure when conditions are cool and dry.

🏜️ Badwater Basin

Famous sight

A vast salt-flat landscape at the valley’s lowest roadside stop.

Fun fact: Badwater Basin is 282 feet below sea level—the lowest point in North America.

Time needed: 30–60 min.

Walking: Easy — 1-mile round trip: boardwalk, then flat salt flats you can walk out on. Look for the “sea level” sign on the cliff behind you. Go early morning — the white salt is blinding and hot by midday.

Recommended: The classic Death Valley stop; go when temperatures are safe.

🔭 Dante’s View

Viewpoint

A high overlook across Badwater Basin and the Panamint Range.

Fun fact: The viewpoint looks down more than a mile toward the salt flats below.

Time needed: 30–45 min at the viewpoint (longer for sunrise/sunset). The drive from Furnace Creek is ~45 min each way.

Walking: No real walk — the view is steps from the parking lot, at 5,475 ft, looking straight down at Badwater Basin. Optional: a short Easy ridge stroll. Reached by a 13-mile paved but steep, winding road off CA-190.

Recommended: The best big-picture view of how the valley, salt flats, and mountains fit together.

Trip combos — stops that fit together

Nearby stops that make one good outing, in the order you reach them on the road.

🧭 Badwater Road half-day

Golden Canyon Trailhead → Artist's Palette → Devils Golf Course → Natural Bridge Trailhead → Badwater Basin

Total ≈ 3.5–4 hrs (plus driving between stops).

Why they fit: All sit along one road — Badwater Road runs south from Furnace Creek and each stop peels off it in order, ending at the valley's lowest point.

🧭 Furnace Creek hub + sunset at Dante's View

Furnace Creek Visitor Center → Harmony Borax Works → Zabriskie Point → Dante's View

Total ≈ 4–5 hrs (do Dante's View last, near sunset).

Why they fit: The first three are all within minutes of Furnace Creek on CA-190; Dante's View Road branches off CA-190 just east of there, so it's one easy add-on — and it's the park's best sunset view.

🧭 West-side half day (Stovepipe Wells area)

Father Crowley Vista Point → Mesquite Flat Sand Dunes → Mosaic Canyon Trailhead → Salt Creek Interpretive Trail

Total ≈ 4–5 hrs (plus driving).

Why they fit: All sit on the western stretch of CA-190 between Towne Pass and Furnace Creek — a straight line of stops, with food and gas at Stovepipe Wells in the middle.

Before you go — read this first

⚠️ Extreme heat: Death Valley is one of the hottest places on Earth — over 120°F in summer. The park itself says: do not hike after 10 AM in summer at low elevations. The best visiting months are November through March. Carry at least 2 liters of water, even on short winter walks.

Road news (mid-October 2026): Scotty's Castle Road / Bonnie Clare Road is CLOSED for flood repairs (no walking in either); Titus Canyon Road is scheduled closed Oct 1, 2026 – Sep 30, 2027; the southern part of Badwater Road (south of Badwater Basin toward Shoshone) is rough — drive with care. The Salt Creek boardwalk reopened in Feb 2026.
