🧭 Trip combos — stops that fit together

🧭 Park Boulevard Classics

Route: West Entrance → Hidden Valley → Barker Dam → Cap Rock → Keys View

Total time: Stop time ≈ 2–2.5 hrs total (plus driving)

Why they fit: Everything sits on Park Boulevard or its one side road, Keys View Road, in the park's northwest — one road, no backtracking.

🧭 East-Side Rock Stars

Route: Skull Rock → Arch Rock → Heart Rock → Cholla Cactus Garden

Total time: Stop time ≈ 1.5–2 hrs total

Why they fit: Drive east on Park Boulevard, then turn onto Pinto Basin Road — the stops come in exactly this order along the two roads, ending further south. (Arch + Heart are one trail stop.)

🧭 North Gate Morning

Route: Oasis of Mara → Fortynine Palms Oasis Trail

Total time: Total ≈ 2.5–3.5 hrs

Why they fit: Both are at Twentynine Palms by the North Entrance, off Highway 62 — do the short oasis stop first, then the bigger hike; the trailhead is a few miles west on Canyon Road. Skip 49 Palms in summer — it is closed June–September.

Spot cards

Northwest

🚪 West Entrance Station

⏱ About 5–10 min (estimate) 🥾 No real walk — ranger booth on Park Boulevard; you stay in the car

Main west gate — pay the entrance fee here.

Fun fact: This is the park's busiest entrance — most visitors come in this way.

Verdict: Best entrance if you're staying in Joshua Tree town; it's the quickest way to the famous rock sights.

ℹ️ Joshua Tree Visitor Center

⏱ 20–30 min (estimate) 🥾 No real walk — indoors in downtown Joshua Tree, just outside the West Entrance

Downtown Joshua Tree — maps, rangers, exhibits.

Fun fact: This visitor center sits in the town of Joshua Tree, right outside the park's west entrance.

Verdict: Stop here first for maps and ranger tips before you drive in.

⛺ Black Rock Campground

⏱ Overnight (a look-around takes 15–20 min) 🥾 No real walk — campground ⛺ Reserve ahead on recreation.gov

Tent and RV camping on the park's west side.

Fun fact: This campground has water faucets — rare in this desert park — and a nature center.

Verdict: Best pick for first-timers; it has water and is close to town.

⛺ Indian Cove Campground

⏱ Overnight (a look-around takes 15–20 min) 🥾 No real walk — campground ⛺ Reserve ahead on recreation.gov

Camp among giant boulders — a climber favorite.

Fun fact: The boulders here are some of the best rock-climbing spots in the park, even for beginners.

Verdict: Recommended for climbers; not the best pick if you mainly want to see big Joshua trees, since this area is more about boulders.

⛺ White Tank Campground

⏱ Overnight (a look-around takes 15–20 min) 🥾 No real walk — campground ⛺ First-come, first-served, $25/night · Closed in summer (about June 1–Aug 28)

Small and scenic, next to Arch Rock.

Fun fact: It's named after a natural white rock basin nearby that fills with rainwater for desert animals.

Verdict: Recommended if you want to walk to Arch Rock at sunrise.

🌵 Skull Rock

⏱ 10–30 min 🥾 No real walk — the rock sits right beside Park Boulevard; optional Easy 1.7-mile nature trail loop (45 min–1 hr)

A rock that looks exactly like a skull. Easy roadside stop.

Fun fact: Rain and wind hollowed out two 'eye sockets' in the rock — that's what makes it look like a skull.

Verdict: A must-stop: it's right by the road and takes 2 minutes.

🌵 Arch Rock

⏱ 30–45 min 🥾 Easy — about 1.2 miles round trip from the parking lot (the loop at the rock itself is about 0.5 mile)

Natural stone arch — short easy trail from White Tank.

Fun fact: The arch formed when softer rock underneath wore away, leaving the harder rock on top.

Verdict: Easy and quick — a great photo stop.

🌵 Barker Dam

⏱ 30–45 min 🥾 Easy — 1.1-mile loop

A dam built by early cattle ranchers; 1.1-mile loop with ancient rock carvings.

Fun fact: The dam was built around 1900 so cattle could drink here — and bighorn sheep still drink here today.

Verdict: One of the best easy hikes; you might spot bighorn sheep.

🌵 Hidden Valley

⏱ 30–45 min 🥾 Easy — 1-mile loop

A hidden valley ringed by giant rocks; easy 1-mile loop.

Fun fact: Cattle rustlers once hid stolen cows in this valley because the rocks hid it from view.

Verdict: A classic easy walk — great for first-time visitors.

🌵 Hall of Horrors

⏱ 30–60 min 🥾 Easy 0.6-mile loop around the rock piles — but the famous slot canyons are tight squeezes and scrambling (Moderate)

Narrow passages between boulders — fun to squeeze through.

Fun fact: It's named for the tight squeezes between the boulders — you have to wiggle through some gaps.

Verdict: Not recommended if you don't like tight spaces — you have to squeeze between boulders.

🌵 Cap Rock

⏱ 15–30 min (estimate) 🥾 Easy — about a 0.4-mile loop trail

Famous balanced rock formation right by the road.

Fun fact: A huge boulder balances on a narrow base — it looks like it should fall over, but it's been there for ages.

Verdict: Right by the road, so it's an easy stop.

🥾 Lost Horse Mine Trailhead

⏱ 2–3 hrs (3–4 hrs if you explore) 🥾 Moderate — 4-mile round trip on an old wagon road (longer loop about 6.5 miles)

4-mile round trip to a real old gold mine.

Fun fact: This mine produced about 10,000 ounces of gold before it closed.

Verdict: Recommended if you like history — it's one of the best-preserved mines in the park.

Northeast

🚪 North Entrance Station

⏱ About 5–10 min (estimate) 🥾 No real walk — ranger booth on Utah Trail, south of Twentynine Palms

North gate near Twentynine Palms.

Fun fact: From here, one road runs all the way south through the park to Cottonwood.

Verdict: Best entrance for Cholla Cactus Garden and the quieter south side.

ℹ️ Oasis of Mara Visitor Center

⏱ 20–30 min (estimate) 🥾 No real walk inside; outside, a short paved path around the oasis (about a 0.5-mile loop, Easy)

By the North Entrance in Twentynine Palms.

Fun fact: The oasis next door has been a water stop for people for thousands of years — the Serrano people called it Mara.

Verdict: Great first stop if you enter from the north; it has a short nature trail to the oasis.

Note: The old Oasis Visitor Center building is permanently closed — the Twentynine Palms visitor center moved downtown to 6533 Freedom Way. The palm oasis and its short trail are still there to visit.

🔭 Ryan Mountain Summit

⏱ 2–3 hrs 🥾 Hard — 3-mile round trip, climbs about 1,000 ft, no shade

360° views from the top — a steep 3-mile round-trip hike.

Fun fact: At 5,457 feet, this is one of the highest viewpoints in the park.

Verdict: Recommended if you like a challenge — but not on a very hot day, since there's no shade on the trail.

⛺ Jumbo Rocks Campground

⏱ Overnight (a look-around takes 15–20 min) 🥾 No real walk — campground ⛺ Reserve ahead on recreation.gov

The classic — huge rock piles right by your tent.

Fun fact: The giant rock piles formed over 100 million years ago, deep underground — erosion uncovered them.

Verdict: The most famous campground in the park — book early, it fills up fast.

⛺ Ryan Campground

⏱ Overnight (a look-around takes 15–20 min) 🥾 No real walk — campground ⛺ Reserve ahead on recreation.gov

Small, quiet campground near Ryan Mountain.

Fun fact: It's one of the smallest campgrounds in the park, so it feels extra quiet at night.

Verdict: Recommended if you want quiet and a short walk to the Ryan Mountain trail.

⛺ Belle Campground

⏱ Overnight (a look-around takes 15–20 min) 🥾 No real walk — campground ⛺ First-come, first-served, $25/night · Closed in summer (about June 1–Aug 28)

Small campground tucked among boulders and Joshua trees.

Fun fact: Joshua Tree is a certified Dark Sky Park, so the stars here are amazing — no city lights.

Verdict: Great for stargazing; small and peaceful.

🌵 Heart Rock

⏱ Add ~15 min on top of Arch Rock (estimate); Arch + Heart together ≈ 45 min–1 hr 🥾 Easy — the same Arch Rock Trail; about 1.7 miles round trip for both (estimate)

A giant boulder shaped like a heart, a short walk past Arch Rock.

Fun fact: It's a giant boulder about 10 feet tall — and at certain times of day, the sun throws a giant heart-shaped shadow.

Verdict: A favorite photo stop — pair it with Arch Rock on the same short trail.

🥾 Ryan Mountain Trailhead

⏱ 2–3 hrs 🥾 Hard — 3-mile round trip, climbs about 1,000 ft, no shade

Start of the steep 3-mile round-trip hike to the summit.

Fun fact: The trail climbs about 1,000 feet — that's like climbing 100 flights of stairs.

Verdict: Recommended for the best views — but bring lots of water and skip it on very hot days.

🥾 Fortynine Palms Oasis Trailhead

⏱ 2–3 hrs 🥾 Moderate — 3-mile round trip; climbs ~300 ft over a ridge, then drops to the oasis. Closed every summer, June 1–Sept 30

3-mile round trip to a hidden palm oasis.

Fun fact: The fan palms at the oasis are California fan palms — the only palm tree native to California.

Verdict: Recommended for a real hidden oasis — just don't go at midday in summer, the trail has almost no shade.

Note: Pin position is approximate — no source pins the parking lot separately; the point marks the trailhead area.

Southwest

🔭 Keys View

⏱ 15–30 min 🥾 No real walk — drive-up overlook; a short paved path from the parking lot to the railing

The big panorama — on clear days you can see all the way to Mexico.

Fun fact: On a really clear day you can see the San Andreas Fault, the Coachella Valley, and Signal Mountain in Mexico.

Verdict: The best sunset spot in the park — go late in the day.

Southeast

🚪 South Entrance (Cottonwood)

⏱ About 5–10 min (estimate) 🥾 No real walk — entrance station on the road up from I-10 (exit 168)

South gate — closest to Interstate 10.

Fun fact: This side of the park is the Colorado Desert — lower, hotter, and full of cholla and ocotillo instead of Joshua trees.

Verdict: Best entrance if you're driving from Phoenix or Palm Springs on I-10; it's also the least crowded.

ℹ️ Cottonwood Visitor Center

⏱ 20–30 min (estimate) 🥾 No real walk — small center on Pinto Basin Road, right by the South Entrance

South end of the park.

Fun fact: Cottonwood Spring nearby was a lifeline for travelers crossing the desert — people have used it for centuries.

Verdict: Good stop for the south end; ask rangers about the spring trail.

⛺ Cottonwood Campground

⏱ Overnight (a look-around takes 15–20 min) 🥾 No real walk — campground ⛺ Reserve ahead on recreation.gov

South-end campground by the visitor center.

Fun fact: This campground is in the Colorado Desert, so you'll see cholla cacti and ocotillo instead of Joshua trees.

Verdict: Recommended if you're driving in from I-10 and want a quiet night.

🌵 Cholla Cactus Garden

⏱ 15–30 min 🥾 Easy — 0.25-mile loop, flat, partly boardwalk

A forest of teddy-bear cholla cacti — look, don't touch!

Fun fact: Cholla pieces break off and stick to anything that brushes them — that's why people call it the 'jumping cactus' (it doesn't really jump).

Verdict: Recommended, but stay on the path — the spines are barbed and hurt.

Before you go — check current conditions

Campgrounds: Black Rock, Indian Cove, Jumbo Rocks, Ryan, and Cottonwood campgrounds take reservations on recreation.gov. Belle and White Tank are first-come, first-served ($25/night) and close in summer (about June 1–Aug 28). No campground has showers; only Black Rock and Cottonwood have water and flush toilets.

Seasonal closures: Fortynine Palms Oasis Trail is closed every summer, June 1–Sept 30. Belle and White Tank campgrounds close in summer (about June 1–Aug 28).

🧭 Trip combos — stops that fit together

🧭 Badwater Road half-day

Route: Golden Canyon Trailhead → Artist's Palette (Artist's Drive loop) → Devils Golf Course → Natural Bridge Trailhead → Badwater Basin

Total time: About 3.5–4 hrs (plus driving between stops)

Why they fit: All five sit along one road — Badwater Road runs south from Furnace Creek and each stop peels off it in order, ending at the lowest point in North America.

🧭 Furnace Creek hub + sunset at Dante's View

Route: Furnace Creek Visitor Center → Harmony Borax Works → Zabriskie Point → Dante's View

Total time: About 4–5 hrs (do Dante's View last, near sunset)

Why they fit: The first three are all within minutes of Furnace Creek on CA-190; Dante's View Road branches off CA-190 just east of there, so the park's best sunset view is one easy add-on.

🧭 West-side half day

Route: Father Crowley Vista Point → Mesquite Flat Sand Dunes → Mosaic Canyon Trailhead → Salt Creek Interpretive Trail

Total time: About 4–5 hrs (plus driving)

Why they fit: All four sit on the western stretch of CA-190 between Towne Pass and Furnace Creek — a straight line of stops with food and gas at Stovepipe Wells in the middle.

Spot cards

North — craters and the far north

🏜️ Ubehebe Crater

⏱ 30–60 min 🥾 Moderate — 1.5-mile rim loop (500 ft up/down); easy option: the view right from the parking lot

A giant crater half a mile wide and about 600 feet deep, blasted out when rising hot magma hit groundwater and flashed it to steam — geologists think it happened only a few thousand years ago, maybe less.

Fun fact: The dark, rusty rocks scattered around the rim are cinders thrown out by the blast. A smaller crater next door is called Little Hebe.

Verdict: Recommended — one of the park's most dramatic sights. The parking-lot view is already amazing; the rim loop is for people with energy to spare.

⛺ Mesquite Spring Campground

⏱ Overnight (a look-around takes 15–20 min) 🥾 No real walk — a campground at 1,800 ft, 2 miles off the main road ⛺ First-come, first-served — no reservations

A quiet 40-site campground in the park's north end, with water and flush toilets. It is the natural base for Ubehebe Crater and the Racetrack.

Fun fact: At 1,800 feet up, nights here are noticeably cooler than on the valley floor at Furnace Creek.

Verdict: Recommended for the north end — peaceful and handy for Ubehebe. First-come, so arrive earlier in the day.

🏜️ Racetrack Playa

⏱ Half to a full day (about 5–6 hrs from Ubehebe) 🥾 No real hike — the famous sailing stones sit on a flat dry lakebed; you stroll from the parking area

A perfectly flat, dry lakebed famous for its 'sailing stones' — rocks that slide across the mud and leave long tracks behind them, with nobody pushing.

Fun fact: Scientists solved the mystery in 2014: on cold nights, thin sheets of ice form on the playa, and light morning winds push the ice — and the rocks frozen in it — slowly across the mud.

Verdict: Recommended ONLY with a high-clearance 4-wheel-drive vehicle and a full day. It is 27 miles of rough gravel road each way, with no cell service. Amazing, but not a casual drive — bring extra water and a full spare tire.

West — canyons, dunes, and Stovepipe Wells

🔭 Father Crowley Vista Point

⏱ 15–20 min (30–45 min with the dirt path) 🥾 No real walk — the view is right by the paved parking lot; optional Easy 1.2-mile dirt path to Padre Point

A viewpoint on CA-190 overlooking Rainbow Canyon, nicknamed 'Star Wars Canyon' after the movies filmed in this desert country.

Fun fact: Military jets train in the canyon below — sometimes flying lower than the viewpoint, so you look DOWN at the planes as they roar past.

Verdict: Recommended — a quick, exciting stop on the west side. Jets are not guaranteed, but the canyon view is.

⛺ Stovepipe Wells Campground

⏱ Overnight 🥾 No real walk — a big, flat campground at sea level ⛺ First-come, first-served — no reservations

A big 190-site campground right next to the small Stovepipe Wells village, which has gas, food, and a store — rare and handy in this huge park.

Fun fact: It is named after a stovepipe that early travelers stuck upright in the sand to mark a hidden water hole so others could find it.

Verdict: Recommended — the handiest base for the sand dunes and Mosaic Canyon. Simple and flat, with the village right there.

🏜️ Mesquite Flat Sand Dunes

⏱ 1–2 hrs 🥾 Easy — up to about 2 miles round trip through sand; there is no marked trail

The park's most famous sand dunes — rolling waves of sand right by CA-190 near Stovepipe Wells. You just park and walk in, climbing as far as you like.

Fun fact: These dunes stood in for the planet Tatooine in the original Star Wars movie.

Verdict: Recommended — go at sunrise or sunset, when the low light makes every ripple glow and the sand is cool enough to enjoy.

🥾 Mosaic Canyon Trailhead

⏱ About 1 hr for a taste; 2.5–3 hrs for the full hike 🥾 Moderate — 4-mile round trip, some scrambling over rocks

A canyon whose walls have been polished smooth as marble by flash floods, with swirled, mosaic-like patterns in the rock. The first pretty narrows arrive in under half a mile.

Fun fact: The 'marble' is really dolomite rock — floodwater carrying gravel has sanded it shiny over thousands of years.

Verdict: Recommended — a beautiful hike that starts paying off fast. Never enter if rain is possible anywhere nearby: flash floods carved this canyon.

Furnace Creek — the park's hub

🥾 Salt Creek Interpretive Trail

⏱ 30 min 🥾 Easy — 0.5-mile flat boardwalk loop, wheelchair accessible

A flat boardwalk over one of the park's rarest things: a living desert stream. It is one of the only homes of the Salt Creek pupfish, a fish found nowhere else on Earth.

Fun fact: The pupfish survive in water saltier than the ocean, in a creek that shrinks to puddles every summer. The boardwalk reopened in February 2026 after flood damage.

Verdict: Recommended — short, flat, and the pupfish are a real-life science surprise. There is almost no shade, so go early.

🏜️ Harmony Borax Works

⏱ 30–45 min 🥾 Easy — 0.4-mile loop past the ruins

The ruins of an 1880s borax refinery right by Furnace Creek — old wagons, tanks, and story signs telling how 'white gold' was mined in the desert.

Fun fact: The famous 20-mule teams hauled double wagons of borax from here 165 miles across the desert to the nearest railroad.

Verdict: Recommended — a quick, easy history stop that kids genuinely like, and it pairs with the visitor center next door.

ℹ️ Furnace Creek Visitor Center

⏱ 45 min – 1 hr 🥾 No real walk — indoor exhibits, a 20-min park film every half hour, rangers, water, air conditioning

The park's main visitor center and the natural first stop: exhibits about how this extreme desert works, plus rangers who know today's road conditions.

Fun fact: The highest temperature ever recorded on Earth — 134°F (57°C) — was measured near here in 1913.

Verdict: Recommended — start here. Ask a ranger about heat safety and which roads are open before you drive anywhere.

⛺ Furnace Creek Campground

⏱ Overnight 🥾 No real walk — a big campground 196 ft below sea level ⛺ Reserve ahead Oct 15 – Apr 15 on recreation.gov; first-come in summer

The park's biggest campground: 136 sites with water, flush toilets, and a dump station, a short walk from the visitor center.

Fun fact: It is the only campground in Death Valley you can reserve ahead — every other one is first-come, first-served.

Verdict: Recommended — the best facilities in the park. Reserve ahead in the cooler season; it fills up.

⛺ Texas Springs Campground

⏱ Overnight 🥾 No real walk — a hillside campground above Furnace Creek ⛺ First-come, first-served — no reservations

A 92-site campground terraced into the hills above Furnace Creek — close to everything, but quieter than its big neighbor down the hill.

Fun fact: No generators are allowed here, which makes it one of the quietest campgrounds in the park. The lower loop is tents only.

Verdict: Recommended — a calmer pick near Furnace Creek. First-come, so arrive earlier in the day in busy months.

🔭 Zabriskie Point

⏱ 20–30 min 🥾 No real hike — a short paved uphill path from the parking lot (about 0.25–0.4 mi round trip, estimate), wheelchair accessible

The park's most photographed viewpoint: a sea of golden, rippled badlands — ancient lakebed hills wrinkled by erosion — glowing at sunrise.

Fun fact: Those wrinkled hills are the dried, tilted bed of a lake that vanished about 5 million years ago.

Verdict: Recommended — a short paved path for a huge payoff, especially at sunrise. Five miles east of Furnace Creek on CA-190.

🥾 Golden Canyon Trailhead

⏱ 1.5–2 hrs 🥾 Moderate — 3-mile round trip to the Red Cathedral, with gentle climbing and a little scrambling

A hike through glowing golden canyon walls to the Red Cathedral, a towering wall of red rock. The canyon narrows and widens like a maze of sunlit stone.

Fun fact: Jawas chased R2-D2 through a canyon like this in the original Star Wars — scenes for it were filmed in Golden Canyon.

Verdict: Recommended — a classic first canyon hike. There is no shade and no water, so carry plenty and start early.

South & East — salt flats and the big view

🏜️ Artist's Palette

⏱ About 1 hr (the drive plus stops) 🥾 No real hike — the colorful hills are right by the parking areas; a short 0.3-mile stroll gets closer

Hills splashed with pink, green, purple, and orange — a natural paint set made by minerals in volcanic rock, seen from the one-way Artist's Drive loop.

Fun fact: The colors are chemistry: iron paints the reds and yellows, manganese makes purple, and weathered volcanic ash turns green.

Verdict: Recommended — Artist's Drive is a 9-mile one-way paved loop off Badwater Road and a trip highlight. Colors glow best in late afternoon. (Vehicles over 25 ft are not allowed on the loop.)

🏜️ Devils Golf Course

⏱ 20–30 min 🥾 No real hike — wander carefully a few yards from the parking area

A wild field of sharp salt pinnacles stretching to the horizon. An old guidebook joked that only the devil could play golf on such rough ground — the name stuck.

Fun fact: The salt spikes are razor sharp — they grow as salty groundwater evaporates, and they crunch and crackle underfoot on hot days.

Verdict: Recommended, with care — like nothing else on Earth, but watch every step and stay near the parking area edges. The salt can cut.

🥾 Natural Bridge Trailhead

⏱ 30–60 min 🥾 Easy — 1-mile round trip on a gravel wash, gentle uphill

A short hike up a gravel canyon to a natural rock bridge spanning overhead — a stone arch carved by floodwater.

Fun fact: The bridge formed when floodwater punched through a narrow fin of rock. Every big flood still reshapes this canyon a little.

Verdict: Recommended — one of the park's best short hikes, and the canyon walls give some shade.

🏜️ Badwater Basin

⏱ 30–60 min 🥾 Easy — 1-mile round trip: boardwalk, then flat salt you can walk out on

The lowest point in North America: 282 feet below sea level. A boardwalk leads onto a vast, white salt flat that looks like snow in the desert.

Fun fact: Look up at the cliff behind the parking lot: a sign high above you marks SEA LEVEL — that is how far below the ocean you are standing.

Verdict: Recommended — a must-see. Go in the early morning; by midday the white salt is blinding and the heat is fierce.

🔭 Dante's View

⏱ 30–45 min at the top (the drive is about 45 min each way from Furnace Creek) 🥾 No real walk — the view is steps from the parking lot; optional short Easy ridge stroll

A viewpoint 5,475 feet up, looking straight down at Badwater Basin — the biggest top-to-bottom view in the park, reached by a steep, winding 13-mile paved road.

Fun fact: On a clear day you can see both the lowest point in North America below you (Badwater) and Mt. Whitney — the highest point in the lower 48 states — on the horizon.

Verdict: Recommended — the park's best sunset view. The road is not for long RVs or trailers, and there is no water at the top.

🧭 Valley Icons (half day, about 3–4 hours)

Route: Tunnel View → Bridalveil Fall → El Capitan Meadow → Yosemite Falls → Happy Isles

Total time: About 3–4 hours

🧭 Glacier Point Road Day (about 4–5 hours plus driving)

Route: Washburn Point → Glacier Point → Sentinel Dome + Taft Point loop (from the shared trailhead)

Total time: About 4–5 hours plus driving

Why they fit: One road in and out. The two viewpoints are on the way to the shared trailhead parking lot, and both hikes start from that same lot, so you park once and do it all.

🧭 Tioga Road High Country (full day, about 6–8 hours with driving)

Route: Olmsted Point → Tenaya Lake → Tuolumne Meadows → Lembert Dome

Total time: About 6–8 hours with driving

Spot cards

Yosemite Valley — the valley floor

ℹ️ Yosemite Valley Welcome Center

⏱ 30–60 min 🥾 No real walk — a building in Yosemite Village, flat and easy to get around

The park's main information hub. Get maps, ask rangers what is open and which waterfalls are flowing, see exhibits, and pick up wilderness permits. The bookstore is here too. Note: the old Yosemite Valley Visitor Center building is permanently closed — this Welcome Center, at 9007 Village Drive and opened in 2022, took over as the main info spot.

Fun fact: The old Valley Visitor Center building is permanently closed. This Welcome Center took over as the park's main information spot in 2022.

Verdict: Recommended — start here on a first visit, especially to get the day's conditions.

🔭 Tunnel View

⏱ 15–30 min 🥾 No real walk — viewpoint by the parking lot

The classic postcard view of the whole valley: El Capitan on the left, Bridalveil Fall on the right, and Half Dome far in the middle. It sits at the east end of the Wawona Tunnel on the road into the valley.

Fun fact: This is the most photographed view in the park. Famous photographer Ansel Adams helped make it famous.

Verdict: Recommended — the single best first look at the valley. Go early or late in the day for fewer crowds and prettier light.

🏜️ Bridalveil Fall

⏱ 30–60 min 🥾 Easy — 0.5-mile round trip, paved with a gentle slope; wheelchair-accessible viewing area

A waterfall about 620 feet tall that flows all year. The short paved trail leads to its base, where you feel the spray.

Fun fact: Its Native name is Pohono, which means 'Spirit of the Puffing Wind' — strong wind can blow the falling water sideways.

Verdict: Recommended — short, easy, and it flows year-round (though it runs low by late fall). Stay behind the barriers: the rocks at the base are very slippery and dangerous. Leashed pets are allowed on this trail, which is rare in Yosemite.

🔭 El Capitan Meadow

⏱ 15–30 min (longer if you stop to watch climbers) 🥾 No real walk — meadow viewpoint by the road, flat ground

📍 Pin position is an estimate — valley floor below El Capitan, near the El Capitan Bridge point.

A giant granite wall about 3,000 feet tall, rising straight up from the valley floor. The meadow in front of it is the classic place to look up and spot tiny climbers on the wall.

Fun fact: El Capitan is one of the biggest granite cliffs in the world. Climbers take days to climb it, sleeping in hammocks hung off the wall.

Verdict: Recommended — bring binoculars if you have them; the climbers look like ants. Note: the El Capitan Bridge area had 15-minute weekday road delays in October 2026 for bridge work — check the conditions page.

🏜️ Yosemite Falls

⏱ 30–60 min 🥾 Easy — 1-mile loop, paved with a gentle slope; the east side of the loop is wheelchair-accessible

📍 Trailhead parking position is an estimate.

The lower part of a waterfall that drops 2,425 feet in all — one of the tallest waterfalls in North America. The easy loop trail goes to its base.

Fun fact: In spring, the spray at the base can soak you. By late summer and fall, the falls can go completely dry — in late October 2026, Yosemite Falls was dry.

Verdict: Recommended in spring and early summer, when the water is roaring. Still a nice easy walk in fall, but do not expect a big waterfall then.

⛺ Camp 4 Campground

⏱ Overnight 🥾 No real walk to the campground itself; it is a walk-in tent campground (you park and carry your gear a short way) ⛺ Reserve on Recreation.gov 1 week ahead, Apr 15–Nov 29 ($10 per person per night, up to 6 per site); first-come Nov–mid-Apr. Tents only — no RVs or trailers, no pets.

A small, famous campground at the foot of the cliffs, right by the valley's climbing areas. It has bear boxes for food storage and tap water.

Fun fact: Camp 4 is on the National Register of Historic Places because modern rock climbing was basically invented here.

Verdict: Recommended for tent campers and climbers who book ahead — but not for RVs, and not if you want quiet (it is busy and social). It can close on short notice when strong wind (over 30 mph) or heavy snow (over 1 foot in 24 hours) is forecast.

⛺ Upper Pines Campground

⏱ Overnight 🥾 No real walk — flat campground roads ⛺ Reserve on Recreation.gov 5 months ahead (released the 15th of the month at 7 am Pacific); $36 per night; open all year; 236 sites; sells out within minutes. RVs up to 35 ft, trailers up to 24 ft.

The biggest campground in the valley, close to Half Dome Village and the Merced River, with tap water and bear boxes. In winter (December to mid-February) usually only the first two loops are open, about 50 campsites.

Fun fact: Upper Pines is the only campground in the park that takes reservations all year round — and one of the hardest to book.

Verdict: Recommended for families who want a valley base camp — set an alarm for the release time.

🥾 Happy Isles

⏱ 30 min to a few hours, depending on the trail 🥾 The trailhead area itself is flat and easy to reach; trails from here, like the Mist Trail to Vernal and Nevada Falls, get steep — check the trail you pick

The trailhead area at the east end of the valley, where the Mist Trail begins, plus a small nature center. 'Happy Isles' are little islands in the Merced River where the water splits around them.

Fun fact: The river here splits into channels around small islands — that is what 'isles' means — and the spot is a favorite place for families to splash around in summer.

Verdict: Recommended as a starting point — even a short walk here gives you river views and the islands. Note (October 2026): the Mist Trail had a partial schedule through October for repair work (open Friday–Sunday and holidays, plus Monday–Thursday 3:30 pm–7 am). Check the conditions page.

Glacier Point & Wawona — views from above and the south

🔭 Glacier Point

⏱ 30–60 min 🥾 Easy — about 0.5-mile round trip from the parking lot to the end of the point, paved

A viewpoint high above the valley with a straight-on view of Half Dome, plus Yosemite Falls and the valley below. It has bathrooms, a gift shop, and a small museum.

Fun fact: Close to a million people visit Glacier Point every year. A hotel stood here from 1917 to 1969, when it burned in a wildfire and was never rebuilt.

Verdict: Recommended — the best Half Dome view you can drive to. Go for sunset if you can. Note: Glacier Point Road is seasonal, open roughly May–November.

🔭 Washburn Point

⏱ 15–30 min 🥾 No real walk — viewpoint by the parking lot, just off Glacier Point Road

A quieter viewpoint a little below and south of Glacier Point, looking at the Clark Range mountains, the Merced River, and Vernal and Nevada Falls.

Fun fact: Washburn Point sits at 7,450 feet, about 250 feet higher than Glacier Point — but it does NOT look over Yosemite Valley, so you cannot see Yosemite Falls from here.

Verdict: Recommended as a calmer stop on the way to Glacier Point. There are no bathrooms here — use Glacier Point's.

🥾 Sentinel Dome Trailhead

⏱ About 2 hrs 🥾 Moderate — 2.2-mile round trip, about 400–460 ft of climbing

The shared parking lot on Glacier Point Road for the Sentinel Dome and Taft Point hikes. The trail climbs through forest to the top of a bare granite dome.

Fun fact: From the top you get a full 360° view — Half Dome, El Capitan, and Yosemite Falls all at once. A famous pine tree once grew out of the very top of the dome and was photographed by Ansel Adams; it died in a drought in 1976 and finally fell in 2003.

Verdict: Recommended — one parking lot, two great hikes. Never climb a dome in a thunderstorm.

🥾 Taft Point Trailhead

⏱ About 2 hrs (3–4 hrs for the combined Sentinel Dome + Taft Point loop, 4.9–5.2 miles) 🥾 Moderate — 2.2-mile round trip

The trail from the same shared parking lot as Sentinel Dome, leading to Taft Point, a cliff-edge viewpoint with deep straight cracks in the granite called the Fissures.

Fun fact: The Fissures are deep cracks you can look down into — but there are no guardrails at the cliff edge, so the drop is right there in front of you.

Verdict: Recommended with caution — amazing views, but keep kids close to you and away from the unfenced edges. People who are scared of heights will not enjoy this one.

🏜️ Mariposa Grove (Grizzly Giant)

⏱ 30–45 min on the flat accessible route to the Grizzly Giant (0.26-mile round trip); 1.5–2 hrs for the Grizzly Giant Loop (2.1 miles) 🥾 Easy on the accessible route; Moderate on the loop

A grove of more than 500 giant sequoias, the biggest grove in the park. The Grizzly Giant is the largest tree in the park. The short Big Trees Loop (0.3 miles, Easy) passes the Fallen Monarch, a tree that fell long ago and you can walk beside.

Fun fact: The Grizzly Giant sprouted around 1,800 years ago, when the Roman Empire was still going. Some trees in the grove are up to about 2,700 years old.

Verdict: Recommended — walking among giants is unforgettable. Arrive early: the Welcome Plaza parking fills by about 10 am in peak season. Park at the Mariposa Grove Welcome Plaza near the South Entrance and take the free shuttle when it is running (about late April through November); Mariposa Grove Road is closed to vehicles about December–March, but stays open to walkers, snowshoers, and skiers.

🏜️ Wawona (historic hotel area)

⏱ 1–2 hrs 🥾 Easy — flat, short walks around the historic buildings

📍 Position is the USGS populated-place point, approximate for the hotel building itself.

A historic mountain resort area with one of California's oldest mountain hotels, the Wawona Hotel, dating to 1879, plus the Pioneer Yosemite History Center nearby, with old cabins and a covered bridge you can walk through.

Fun fact: Before cars existed, visitors arrived here by stagecoach — and the hotel has been welcoming them ever since the 1870s.

Verdict: Recommended — a gentle, historic stop on the way in from the south entrance, good for a meal or a stroll. The hotel area itself needs no booking (the Wawona Campground nearby is on reservations April–October).

Tioga Road & High Country — the high road east

🔭 Olmsted Point

⏱ 15–30 min 🥾 No real walk for the main view — pull off Tioga Road and look; a short 0.125-mile trail leads to a small granite dome with an even clearer view

📍 Other sources place this point within about 150 m — a small uncertainty, not a published exact point.

A viewpoint looking south into Tenaya Canyon, with the north side of Half Dome, Clouds Rest, and Tenaya Lake in the distance, plus strange boulders left behind by glaciers scattered on the granite slopes.

Fun fact: It is named after Frederick Law Olmsted, the man who designed New York's Central Park. He also helped protect Yosemite as a park.

Verdict: Recommended — one of the best roadside views in the high country, and easy for everyone.

🏜️ Tenaya Lake

⏱ 30 min to a few hours 🥾 Easy — the lake is right by the road, with flat shoreline walks and picnic areas (one picnic area is wheelchair accessible)

The largest natural lake in Yosemite, a clear blue lake right beside Tioga Road. You can picnic, swim (the water is very cold!), canoe, kayak, sail, and fish with a license. Trailheads nearby lead to Cathedral Lakes, Clouds Rest, Half Dome, and the valley floor.

Fun fact: The lake is named for Chief Tenaya. Its older name, Pieweack, means 'Lake of the Shining Rocks.'

Verdict: Recommended — the perfect picnic and paddle stop on a Tioga Road drive.

🏜️ Tuolumne Meadows

⏱ 1–3 hrs for meadow walks (a full day for longer hikes) 🥾 Easy for the meadow walks (the Soda Springs Trail is 1 mile, flat, Easy, and passes a fizzy natural spring); harder trails lead into the backcountry

📍 Visitor-center position is an estimate — village complex location.

A wide, high meadow about 8,600 feet up, with a small visitor center, a store, a lodge, and a campground — all seasonal and much more rustic than anything in the valley. It is the gateway to the park's backcountry.

Fun fact: Tuolumne Meadows is the largest high-mountain meadow in the Sierra Nevada. It was carved by a glacier more than 2,000 feet thick and two and a half miles long.

Verdict: Recommended — the high country's wide-open heart. Walk slowly on your first day up there, because the thin air at 8,600 feet can make you feel tired faster. Note: facilities and the campground are summer-only; the campground was already closed for the season by late October 2026.

🥾 Lembert Dome Trailhead

⏱ 2–3 hrs for the dome hike (the longer Dog Lake loop is about 5 miles, 2–4 hrs) 🥾 Moderate — 2.8-mile round trip on the steeper path; the back side of the dome is a gentler walk

📍 Parking-lot position is an estimate — lot sits right off Tioga Road at the foot of the dome.

The trailhead parking lot right off Tioga Road at the foot of Lembert Dome, a granite dome rising about 800 feet above the meadows. The top has a grand view west over the meadows and the Cathedral Range. The lot fills early in the day.

Fun fact: The dome is a rock shape smoothed on one side and steep on the other by a glacier sliding over it — scientists call that shape a 'roche moutonnée.'

Verdict: Recommended for confident walkers — but the bare rock is slick in rain or wind, and there is no shelter from wind up there. Note (October 2026): the trail between the wilderness center and Lembert Dome was closed for construction, with an alternate trail available. Check the conditions page.

Hetch Hetchy — the reservoir valley

🏜️ O'Shaughnessy Dam

⏱ 30–60 min 🥾 Easy — a flat walk across the dam; the trailhead for Wapama Falls is right there

A big concrete dam that holds back Hetch Hetchy Reservoir, which supplies drinking water to San Francisco. You can walk across it and look down the valley. There are two parking lots: one before the dam (with restrooms, about a 0.25-mile walk to the trailhead) and one after the dam (a shorter walk).

Fun fact: The valley behind the dam was once called Yosemite's twin. John Muir fought hard to save it, but the dam was finished in 1923 and flooded it anyway.

Verdict: Recommended — a short, easy stop with big views and big history. Note: Hetch Hetchy Road is open sunrise to sunset only, and may close or require chains for ice and snow November–March. Vehicles or trailers longer than 25 feet or wider than 8 feet are not allowed on the road. There is no public transport to Hetch Hetchy, and no cafes at the entrance — the nearest food is in Groveland.

🥾 Wapama Falls Trailhead

⏱ About 2 hrs (NPS figure; some guides say 2.5–3.5 hrs) 🥾 Moderate — 5-mile round trip, 507 ft of climbing

The trail starts at the dam, goes through a short tunnel blasted out of granite, then follows the reservoir shoreline with moderate ups and downs to Wapama Falls.

Fun fact: Wapama Falls is about 1,000 feet tall — and before the dam raised the water level, it was about 400 feet taller (guide-sourced, not an NPS figure).

Verdict: Recommended — best later in the season, when high water is not flooding the bridges. In spring and early summer expect heavy mist and spray from the falls. Watch for rattlesnakes and poison oak. Pets are not allowed on this trail, and swimming and boating in the reservoir are not allowed because it is drinking water. Note: the trail can be closed in winter and early spring when high water floods the bridges.

Spot cards

Painted Desert — the north end

ℹ️ Painted Desert Inn

⏱ 30–45 min 🥾 No real walk — the museum is indoors; the Kachina Point viewpoint is right behind the building

A 1920s adobe inn turned museum and National Historic Landmark, sitting on the rim above the Painted Desert. Inside are exhibits on the inn's Route 66 days, the Civilian Conservation Corps, and big murals by Hopi artist Fred Kabotie.

Fun fact: The first version of the inn was nicknamed the 'Stone Tree House' because it was built partly out of petrified wood. In the 1930s the Civilian Conservation Corps rebuilt it, and famous architect Mary Colter remodeled it for the Fred Harvey Company.

Verdict: Recommended — the best first stop in the north. The murals alone are worth it, and the view behind the building is the classic Painted Desert panorama.

🔭 Kachina Point

⏱ 15–30 min 🥾 No real walk — the viewpoint is steps behind the Painted Desert Inn; the Painted Desert Rim Trail starts here

The main overlook for the red part of the Painted Desert — rolling badlands banded in red, pink, lavender, and gray, stretching to the horizon.

Fun fact: 'Kachina' (katsina) refers to the spirit beings of the Hopi people, whose lands lie just west of the park. The Hopi word for the sun, 'Tawa,' names the next overlook south.

Verdict: Recommended — the postcard view of the Painted Desert. Come in late afternoon, when the colors are strongest.

🔭 Tawa Point

⏱ 15–20 min for the overlook; 30–45 min if you walk the Rim Trail to Kachina Point 🥾 No real walk for the overlook itself — it is by the parking lot. The Painted Desert Rim Trail from here to Kachina Point is Easy, about 1.2 miles round trip along the rim 📍 Pin position estimated

Another rim overlook over the same rainbow badlands, usually quieter than Kachina Point, and the starting point of the Painted Desert Rim Trail.

Fun fact: 'Tawa' is the Hopi word for the sun — a good name, because this is one of the best sunset-watching spots in the park.

Verdict: Recommended — same colors as Kachina with fewer people, plus the easy rim walk if you want it.

🔭 Chinde Point

⏱ 15–30 min 🥾 No real walk — the overlook and picnic tables are by the parking lot 📍 Pin position estimated

A west-facing overlook just past the Painted Desert Inn, with picnic tables — the nicest lunch stop in the north end.

Fun fact: 'Chinde' is a Navajo word for a ghost or spirit — a fitting name for this quiet, empty-looking stretch of badlands.

Verdict: Recommended — bring lunch and eat it with a Painted Desert view. Best in late afternoon light.

Central — pueblos, rock art, and log forests

🏜️ Puerco Pueblo

⏱ 20–30 min 🥾 Easy, 0.3-mile paved loop

The low stone walls of a 100-room ancestral Puebloan village, lived in around 1100–1400 CE, plus petroglyphs on boulders just below the rim of a shallow canyon.

Fun fact: One petroglyph here works as a calendar: on the summer solstice, a beam of sunlight hits it in a special way, marking the longest day of the year.

Verdict: Recommended — short, flat, and the most human story in the park. Great for kids.

🏜️ Newspaper Rock

⏱ 15–30 min 🥾 No real walk — a short paved path from the parking lot to the overlook platform; the rocks themselves are viewed from a distance and hiking closer is not allowed

A boulder face covered in more than 650 petroglyphs — pictures pecked into dark rock by different peoples between about 1000 and 1500 CE, some up to 2,000 years old. The platform has telescopes for a closer look.

Fun fact: The carvings are like a newspaper written over centuries: animals, people, spirals, and handprints, each group adding its own messages to the same rock.

Verdict: Recommended — one of the park's most special stops. Bring binoculars if you have them.

🔭 Jasper Forest Overlook

⏱ 15–20 min 🥾 No real walk — the overlook is by the parking lot. (An unmarked wander into the badlands is possible but is not a maintained trail.)

A panoramic view over a valley packed with petrified wood — one of the park's biggest concentrations, spread out below the overlook like a forest that fell over.

Fun fact: Some logs here still show their root systems — proof that these trees grew right here instead of washing in from somewhere else.

Verdict: Recommended — the widest 'forest of stone' view in the park, and quieter than the southern trails.

🥾 Crystal Forest Trail

⏱ 30–45 min 🥾 Easy, 0.75-mile paved loop (NPS)

A paved loop past colorful logs that glimmer with quartz crystals. Long ago this area held logs with clear quartz and purple amethyst in their cracks — the best pieces were taken by souvenir hunters before the park was protected.

Fun fact: The sparkle comes from quartz crystals that grew inside cracks in the logs while they were buried underground.

Verdict: Recommended — the easiest, prettiest log walk in the park, and great for close-up photos.

🏜️ Agate House

⏱ 30–45 min for the Agate House trail alone; about 2 hrs for the combined Long Logs + Agate House loop 🥾 Easy, 1.2-mile round trip to the house from the Long Logs trailhead (first stretch paved, then a flat desert path) 📍 Pin position estimated

A partially reconstructed 8-room pueblo built almost entirely of petrified wood, lived in about 700–1,000 years ago, at the end of a quiet desert trail.

Fun fact: The builders sealed the petrified-wood walls with mud mortar — and the house is still standing centuries later. Archaeologists have since found other petrified-wood structures in the park.

Verdict: Recommended — the only building made of stone trees you will ever see. Do not sit, stand, or climb on it.

Rainbow Forest & Blue Mesa — the south end

🥾 Blue Mesa

⏱ 45 min – 1 hr 🥾 Moderate, 1-mile paved loop — the path drops steeply down into the badlands and climbs back up (about 237 ft of climbing), with no shade

A trail down into blue, purple, and gray striped badlands made of soft bentonite clay, with petrified logs scattered through the hills. A 3.5-mile loop road with overlooks circles the mesa if you would rather drive it.

Fun fact: The clay swells when wet and shrinks when dry, which is what sculpts the weird cone-shaped hills — and logs lying on top act like umbrellas, protecting little clay pedestals underneath them.

Verdict: Recommended — the park's most alien-looking walk. Do it in the cooler part of the day, carry water, and take the steep climb slowly.

🏜️ Agate Bridge

⏱ 15–20 min 🥾 No real walk — a short paved path (about 0.2 mi round trip) from the parking lot to the viewpoint

A 110-foot petrified log spanning a gully like a bridge, held up by a concrete support beam placed under it in 1917 so it would not collapse.

Fun fact: It was once called the 'most noted single petrified log in the world.' Both ends of the log are still buried in the sandstone where it fossilized.

Verdict: Recommended — a quick, wow photo stop. Never walk on the log itself.

ℹ️ Rainbow Forest Museum

⏱ 45 min – 1 hr 🥾 No real walk — indoor exhibits, a bookstore, and a free orientation film shown all day

The south-end museum, with petrified wood, fossils, and prehistoric-animal displays, plus information and restrooms. The Giant Logs and Long Logs trails start right behind it.

Fun fact: The Rainbow Forest area was the park's original headquarters from 1906 (when it was a national monument) until 1962.

Verdict: Recommended — the best place to learn how a tree turns to stone before you walk the trails behind the building.

🥾 Giant Logs Trail

⏱ 20–30 min 🥾 Easy, 0.4-mile paved loop (NPS) behind the museum — but there are stairs, so it is not fully step-free

A short loop past some of the biggest, most colorful logs in the park, including the giant 'Old Faithful' log.

Fun fact: 'Old Faithful' is a log nearly 10 feet wide at its base and weighing about 44 tons. An early park superintendent named it because he wanted his park to have its own Old Faithful, like Yellowstone's.

Verdict: Recommended — the best short walk in the park. Do it right after the museum.

🥾 Long Logs Trail

⏱ About 1 hr for Long Logs alone; about 2 hrs for the combined Long Logs + Agate House loop 🥾 Easy, 2.6-mile loop combined with Agate House (NPS); the first 0.5 mi is paved, then it turns to a dirt desert path

A loop through the park's largest concentration of petrified logs — a great logjam of trunks piled criss-cross, some of them more than 100 feet long.

Fun fact: These logs piled up because a river flood swept whole trees downstream about 225 million years ago, when this desert was a green floodplain full of tall conifers, crocodile-like reptiles, and early dinosaurs.

Verdict: Recommended — the best log walk if you have an hour or two. Combine it with Agate House for the full loop.

Before you go — check current conditions

🚫 Taking petrified wood is ILLEGAL. Removing any petrified wood, fossil, rock, or artifact from the park is against the law and carries fines. If you want a piece to keep, buy one from a shop outside the park (sold legally from private land).

The park road closes at night. Gates close every evening — the park is not open 24 hours, so you must leave by closing time (summer about 7 PM, spring/fall about 6 PM, winter about 5 PM, Mountain Standard Time year-round; Arizona does not do Daylight Saving Time). The park is open every day except Thanksgiving and Christmas Day.

Monsoon flash-flood season: June 15 to September 30. Sudden thunderstorms can bring lightning and flash floods — never cross a flooded wash or road. Summer days go over 100°F. Best visiting months are spring and fall.

Water and height: The park sits around 5,400–5,800 feet up, so the sun is stronger than it feels — wear sunscreen and carry water. Drinking water is only at the Painted Desert complex (north end) and the Rainbow Forest Museum (south end), NOT at stops along the road.

Stay on trails and off the ruins: The desert soil is fragile — plants grow by the inch and are destroyed by the foot. Do not climb on pueblo walls, touch petroglyphs, or walk on petrified logs.
